# Rights and references

Use this guide when finding or cataloging material for MSU campus models. See the [README](../README.md) for the current asset catalog and the [worker guide](./WORKER_GUIDE.md) for version tracking.

## Link and catalog first

A publicly viewable image, social post, or map can help locate a reference. Public visibility does not establish permission to reproduce it, turn it into a texture, redistribute it, or publish it in a render. A public repository does not supply that permission either.

Start with the source page and a record of what it shows. Do not copy a photograph into the repository, model, texture set, or contact sheet until the intended use is covered by a recorded license or permission. Any public use needs rights covering that public release. Do not publish raw copyrighted reference images. When rights are unclear, link the public source page for review and mark the use unresolved.

Do not scrape gated content, bypass access controls, or treat someone else's login as permission to collect material. If a reference requires access you do not have, record the gap and request an authorized source.

## Record enough to check the source

For each reference, record:

- Source title, creator or publisher when known, and a stable public source-page URL
- Date captured or published when available, and date checked
- Building or asset shown, view direction where known, and relevant visible features
- How building identity was checked, including any foreground/background distinction
- The specific feature, measurement, or modeling decision the source supports
- Original measurement units, coordinate information where relevant, and limitations
- Rights status for the intended use, with the supporting license or permission link
- Required attribution, restrictions, or unresolved questions
- For an authorized stored copy: public-safe asset alias, version, and SHA-256 of the actual file. Keep the exact private storage identity and path in the owner-controlled private handoff

Use `unknown` when information is missing. Do not fill gaps with plausible-looking dates, measurements, rights statements, or hashes. Keep public source links and public-safe asset aliases. Never put private storage IDs or links, local machine paths, account emails, credentials, tokens, or signed download URLs in public records.

## Verify the building visually

Inspect the image itself. Compare the building's shape, roof, distinctive facade details, entrances, and surroundings with other suitable evidence. A caption, filename, alt text, or embedded location field can be wrong or describe a different part of the frame.

When an image shows several structures, identify which visible structure supports the claim. Check dates and renovation stages before treating conflicting views as geometry errors. Record uncertainty and open a reference-gap issue when the available evidence cannot support a confident decision.

Reference quality and usage rights are separate checks. A reliable photo can still lack permission for copying or texture use. A licensed image can still show the wrong building or an outdated state.

## Keep people and private information out of the work

Prefer references without identifiable people or private interiors. Avoid collecting, reproducing, or retaining images of minors, private spaces, and precise personal metadata. Do not record personal contact details, faces, vehicle identifiers, or location trails just because they are visible in a source.

If incidental people or private information prevent safe use, choose another source or flag the material for review. Before sharing an approved image derivative, inspect both the pixels and metadata. Removing metadata alone does not remove identifying details from the image, and cropping or blurring does not establish reproduction rights.

## Separate storage and access

The repository is public. The GitHub Project and owner-controlled source storage are separate and private unless their owners authorize a change. Public repository access does not grant access to working binaries, source archives, private references, or the Project. Check authorized reviewers' existing access through private coordination and report missing access without exposing the private link, granting it, or promising it.

Keep heavy source material and model binaries in owner-controlled private storage. Record public-safe asset aliases, versions, and hashes in the catalog so authorized reviewers can identify the exact material through the separate private handoff. Do not choose a repository license or apply a blanket license to third-party material as part of reference intake.

If rights, identity, dimensions, or access remain unresolved, say exactly what is missing and which work is blocked. Continue only the parts that do not depend on that material.
